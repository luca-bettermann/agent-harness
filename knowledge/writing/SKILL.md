---
name: writing
description: Writing rules for prose people read: docs, READMEs, notes, wiki pages, issues, comments and reports. Use whenever writing or editing such text.
---

- **Write for the next colleague.** Assume technical competence, not project history. Purpose first, then what to do, edge cases last.
- **Short and plain.** Short sentences, everyday words, no filler, recaps or hedging. Define an acronym or unavoidable term once.
- **One term per concept.** The same word for the same thing throughout, and the tracker's vocabulary for work items.
- **One page per topic.** A README fits on one screen: what it is, how to start, links. Details go to their own files; state each rule once and link it elsewhere.
- **Structure over prose.** Tables for comparisons, lists for steps, headings people can scan. A diagram (Mermaid, inline) when it explains better than text.
- **Current state only.** Describe how things are now; history and rulings belong in git, ADRs or the tracker. Keep a reason only where a choice would otherwise look arbitrary.
- **Concrete.** Real paths, commands and values; dates instead of "recently" or "the new server". Mark an unverified value and who can confirm it.
- **Warnings before risk.** A physical, destructive or security-sensitive step gets its warning before the step, stating the risk and the safe action.
